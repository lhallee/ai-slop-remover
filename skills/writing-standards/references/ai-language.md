# AI-associated language watchlist

Last reviewed: October 2026; storytelling guidance added, lexical sources unchanged.

Use this reference as an editing aid, not an authorship detector. A listed word does not prove that text was generated by AI. Prefer the ordinary, specific word, and keep a flagged term when it is the established technical term or the most accurate choice.

The watchlist is informed by corpus studies of lexical overrepresentation in LLM-assisted writing:

- [COLING 2025: Why Does ChatGPT "Delve" So Much? Exploring the Sources of Lexical Overrepresentation in Large Language Models](https://aclanthology.org/2025.coling-main.426/)
- [Science Advances 2025: Delving into LLM-assisted writing in biomedical publications through excess vocabulary](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12219543/)
- [PNAS 2025: Do LLMs write like humans? Variation in grammatical and rhetorical styles](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11874169/)

Refresh the list during suite maintenance. Do not browse for a new list during each writing task.

## Structural editing comes first

Removing flagged words can leave a lifeless or formulaic piece. For blogs and social posts, read `storytelling.md` and review the angle, stakes, progression, and payoff before polishing language. Common failures include an announcement before any reader-relevant problem, an unexplained number used as a hook, disconnected facts, a result buried under routine caveats, and an ending that says only that the future is exciting.

Replace those patterns with the specific story the evidence supports. Preserve genuine enthusiasm, humor, and authorial voice. Do not replace them with uniformly cautious prose or a reusable influencer cadence. These are editorial judgments, not additional keyword bans or evidence of AI authorship.

## Default-avoid terms

Remove these in ordinary prose unless the use is literal or technically necessary:

- `delve`
- metaphorical `tapestry`
- `realm`
- vague `showcase`
- vague `underscore`
- `intricate`
- `pivotal`
- `groundbreaking`
- metaphorical `load-bearing`

Do not replace one decorative term with another. Delete the phrase or state the concrete action, result, or consequence.

## Context-review terms

Inspect these terms rather than replacing them automatically:

- `provenance`
- `robust`
- `significant`
- `comprehensive`
- `crucial`
- `landscape`
- `leverage`
- `foster`
- `align`
- `potential`

Keep `provenance` for the established concept of data or artifact lineage. Otherwise prefer `source`, `history`, `lineage`, `attribution`, or `audit trail`.

Replace `robust` with the property that was tested, such as stable across seeds, insensitive to a threshold, or accurate under a named distribution shift. Use `significant` only with its intended statistical or material meaning. Replace `crucial` with the consequence. Prefer `use` to `leverage` when both mean the same thing.

## Construction watchlist

Review and usually remove:

- Empty transitions such as `it is important to note` and `it is worth noting`.
- Generic openings about a rapidly changing world or field.
- Repeated contrast templates such as `not just X, but Y` or `not only X, but also Y` when there is no real correction.
- Participial endings that restate the sentence with `highlighting`, `demonstrating`, `showcasing`, or similar verbs.
- Adjective triads that add tone without adding facts.
- Repeated sentence openings or paragraph shapes.
- Symmetrical bold-label lists used for simple linear information.
- Conclusions that restate the introduction.
- Headings, tables, examples, summaries, and background sections that add no information.
- Generic optimistic endings and calls to imagine future possibilities.

Use the advisory checker in `../scripts/audit_language.py` to locate mechanical signals. Review meaning, rhythm, structure, and information gain yourself.
